# Physical Microfont Dista atlas measurements — 2026-10-18

The physical Intel iGPU (`8086:4680`) completed three benchmark matrices: **288 rendered atlases**, all with hardware timestamps, correct geometry counts, and successful cleanup. Ordinary `make iso` includes the benchmark; GPU diagnostics exposes `dista-bench 7`. It runs in the background only when selected. Both rebuilt images were booted on the rig; SMB distribution was skipped.

Each atlas contains **95 printable Basic Latin slots** (`U+0020..U+007E`, including blank space), one weight at a time. Glyphs are 96 × 176 px; each 1536 × 1056 RGBA8 destination occupies 6,488,064 bytes. GLSL constructs positions and the existing triangle renderer fills the body. Timing ends at a retired RGBA8 back buffer; window creation, readback and presentation are excluded.

The phase build exposes the four existing GPU timestamps, partitioning clear/setup, body draw and final writeback without adding commands. Vertex-shader and raster work overlap within the body interval.

Reused-buffer wall times still vary with the live system: summed Fine/direct warm medians changed from 25.233 to 35.877 ms between runs while GPU time stayed almost unchanged.

**Clear/setup dominates at 8.172865 ms per atlas**, versus a **9.271 µs** median final writeback. More markers or controlled variants are needed to separate fullscreen clearing from other commands in that interval. Draft → High adds 55.2% triangles but only about 4.0% complete GPU time. Faster link checks consistently help the body draw.

Next experiments: a clear-only baseline, alternate GPU target initialization, and smaller or differently packed atlas targets. First-use allocation also initializes the destination on the CPU before GPU clearing; admitting a single GPU overwrite could remove that redundant work. These improvements have not been measured.

All 36 lifecycles released the mesh, lease and frame. Every phase sample sums to the complete interval within 1 ns of rounding at the 19.2 MHz timestamp frequency. No benchmark errors, missing timestamps or presentation copies occurred. Host intervals use calibrated TSC reads; nanosecond units do not imply nanosecond accuracy.
