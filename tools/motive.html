Small rules. Good surprises.

Motif makes tiny, repeatable pixel patterns from a seed. A structured generator draws the shape, a curated palette supplies the color, and symmetry ties it all together.

Three families

  • Flowers: layered radial petals and little geometric centers.
  • Snowflakes: branching arms and crystalline details. Six arms is the classic starting point.
  • Mosaics: square-periodic ornaments with matching opposite edges. Symmetry changes the inner ornament; the outer tile stays square.

Find your sweet spot

Use Collection to browse seeds, Repeat to test a pattern, and Compare to hold a single seed steady while changing density and detail. Click a comparison square to apply those raw settings.

Keep it balanced limits density to 20–82, detail to 10–78, and contrast to 20–90. Switch it off to explore the extremes. Aesthetic quality is subjective; these are useful guardrails, not a promise that every combination will be your favorite.

A small, portable tool

All shapes are computed in JavaScript into a 32 × 32 RGBA array on the CPU. Canvas 2D displays and exports those pixels. No WebGL, remote requests, libraries, fonts, or image assets are used. Browser display compositing may still use its normal acceleration.

The HTML works offline. Download a native PNG, a sheet with no gaps, a tiled PNG, a reproducible recipe, or the standalone generator.
